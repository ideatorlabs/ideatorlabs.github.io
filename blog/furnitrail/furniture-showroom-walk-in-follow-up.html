Why furniture showrooms lose walk-in buyers, and how to follow up

What to capture at the door, what to send, and when.

A walk-in buyer who says “we’ll come back with family” is lost after they leave, not on the floor, when the store has no number, no note of what they liked, and nobody owns the follow-up. Three habits fix most of it. Note the buyer, the pieces and who else decides before they leave; send a shortlist on WhatsApp the same day; and keep a daily list of who to follow up, with a reason logged for every lost sale.

Key takeaways

  • Before the buyer leaves, note their name and number (with permission), the pieces they liked, the size and budget, and who else decides.
  • Send a shortlist on WhatsApp the same day, with photos, dimensions and prices that the buyer can forward to family.
  • Every follow-up should add something new. Log one reason for every lost sale, and read your own numbers each week.

Why do walk-in buyers leave without buying?

A sofa, bed or dining set is a big, shared decision. Buyers want to check the size against the room, see the fabric in daylight, compare prices and ask the family. Leaving to decide is normal. What decides the sale is whether the store stays in touch while that decision is being made.

Furniture and flooring trade press in the US says the same: visitors who didn’t buy are won back by planned, consistent follow-up across phone, text and email, logged so each salesperson is accountable (FCNews column, January 2025; US trade press, no figures given). We found no Indian study with numbers on this, so we don’t quote any.

What should staff note before the buyer leaves?

  1. Name and mobile number, with permission. Say why you are asking: “I’ll send you photos and prices of what you liked on WhatsApp.” Use the number only for that.
  2. The pieces they liked. A photo of each, with the model name.
  3. Size and room. Room size if they know it, and the size of the piece they looked at.
  4. Rough budget. So the shortlist can include an option within it.
  5. Who else decides, and when. “Spouse, this weekend” tells you when to follow up.
  6. Which salesperson served them. One name owns the follow-up.

On permission: India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025, with an eighteen-month period for phased compliance. They require a separate consent notice that is clear and easy to understand and explains the specific purpose of collecting the data (PIB explainer, 17 November 2025). Telling the buyer plainly what the number is for, and using it only for that, is a good habit now. This is not legal advice.

What should you send, and how?

Send a shortlist the same day, while the visit is fresh. Keep it to two or three options: the piece they liked, one within budget, and one step up. Each needs a photo, dimensions and price, plus a way to book a second visit. Make it something the buyer can forward to the family member who wasn’t there.

You don’t need paid software to send it. The WhatsApp Business app is free to download (WhatsApp Help Center). WhatsApp’s click-to-chat links open a chat with any number in the format https://wa.me/<number>, and can carry a ready message with ?text= (WhatsApp Help Center: click to chat). Staff can keep a few ready messages and send them from the store’s own number.

Example message (adapt it to your store): “Hi, thanks for visiting today. Here are the three sofas you liked, with sizes and prices. Happy to hold the grey fabric swatch for you to see in daylight. Would Saturday suit for a visit with family?”

When should you follow up?

Follow up when the buyer said the decision would happen, and add something new each time. A note that only says “just checking” gives the buyer nothing to reply to.

A starting schedule (an example, not a measured rule; adjust it once you see what works in your store):

  • Same day: the shortlist.
  • Day 2: answer the open question: delivery date, fabric options, or a similar piece within budget.
  • Day 7: invite them in with family, with a specific day and time.
  • Day 14: a last, short note. If they say no, ask what decided it, thank them and stop.

WhatsApp Business app or API: what does it cost a showroom?

The WhatsApp Business app is free to download and is enough for staff sending one-to-one follow-ups from the store’s own number. The WhatsApp Business Platform (the API, used by most automation tools) is charged per delivered message. Since 1 October 2026, Meta also charges for service messages beyond a free tier of 1,000 a month per business phone number (Meta developer docs: pricing). The API makes sense when you send in volume or automatically; for a showroom’s daily follow-ups, the app and click-to-chat links usually do the job.

How do you know if follow-up is working?

Log the outcome of every walk-in with one reason: bought, price, delivery time, fabric or size, family said no, or bought elsewhere. Then look at four counts each week: walk-ins noted, shortlists sent, follow-ups done on the day they were due, and sales. Compare your own store month to month. The lost reasons tell you what to change: price, range, delivery promise or the follow-up itself.

Questions owners ask

What should a showroom do when a buyer says “we’ll come back with family”?

Before they leave, note their number with permission, the pieces they liked and when the family will decide. Send a shortlist with photos, sizes and prices on WhatsApp the same day so they can share it, then follow up on the day they named.

Do I need the WhatsApp Business API to follow up with walk-ins?

No. The WhatsApp Business app is free to download, and click-to-chat links let staff send ready messages from the store’s own number. The API is charged per message and suits high-volume or automatic sending.

How many times should a showroom follow up?

There is no measured rule for Indian showrooms. A common starting point is the same day, then around day 2, day 7 and day 14, each with something new. Stop after a clear no and log the reason.
