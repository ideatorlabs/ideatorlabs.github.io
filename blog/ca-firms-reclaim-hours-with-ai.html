Where CA firms lose hours every week, and how AI gives them back

The time goes into data work, not advice. Here is where it hides and how to win it back one task at a time.

In this article
  1. Where the hours go
  2. What AI can take over, and what it can’t
  3. How to measure it in your own firm
  4. How to start
  5. How we can help

Most CA firms lose their weekly hours in four places: matching purchase books against GSTR-2B, chasing and sorting client documents, typing the same entries into Tally and then again on portals, and building month-end reports by hand. AI and simple automation can do the reading, matching and typing, so your team only reviews what doesn’t match. The judgment calls, the advice and the sign-off stay with your people.

Key takeaways

  • The lost hours are in data work (matching, sorting, re-typing), not in advice.
  • Software can read bills, match them against GSTR-2B and prepare entries for Tally. People check only the mismatches and anything the software is unsure about.
  • Start with one task. Time it today, automate only that, then compare before you pick the next one.

Where the hours go

  • GST matching. Every registered business gets a GSTR-2B statement built from what its suppliers filed. For monthly filers the portal generates it on the 14th of the following month, and the GST portal’s own FAQ asks taxpayers to reconcile it with their books (GST portal FAQ on GSTR-2B). In practice someone checks purchase entries against it line by line and follows up on every mismatch.
  • Chasing documents. Clients send bills as WhatsApp photos, scanned PDFs and email attachments. Someone has to download, rename, file and read each one before any real work starts.
  • Typing the same thing twice. Bank statements and bills are keyed into Tally by hand, and parts of the same data are keyed again on government portals.
  • Month-end reports. The same MIS sheets and client status updates are put together by hand every month.
  • More clients means more hiring. When every return needs the same manual steps, the only way to take on more clients is to hire more people for the same work.

What AI can take over, and what it can’t

  • Reading documents. Software can read bills and bank statements and pull out the GSTIN, invoice number, date and amounts. It makes mistakes, more so on blurry photos, so anything it is unsure about goes to a person.
  • Matching. Simple rules can compare your purchase register with GSTR-2B and list only the mismatches for your team to look at.
  • Posting entries. Once checked, entries can be imported into your accounting software instead of being typed in one by one.
  • What stays with your people. Deciding what to claim, advising the client, talking to the department and signing off. AI helps with the preparation, not the judgment.

How to measure it in your own firm

For example (made-up numbers, to show the sum): say three juniors each spend two days a month on GSTR-2B matching. That is six person-days a month. If software does the first pass and they only review the mismatches, you time the new process for a month and compare it with those six days. The difference is your real saving, measured in your firm, not taken from a survey.

How to start

  1. Pick one task. For most firms that is GSTR-2B matching or document intake.
  2. Time it for a month the way it runs today.
  3. Automate only that task, with a person still reviewing the output.
  4. Compare the hours, then decide whether it is worth moving to the next task.
