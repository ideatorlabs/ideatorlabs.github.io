How CA firms miss GST, TDS and ROC due dates, what it costs, and how to stop

What a late GST, TDS or ROC filing costs, and how firms keep every return on time.

In this article

CA firms miss GST, TDS and ROC due dates when the only record of what is pending is one manager’s Excel sheet and WhatsApp chats that nobody else can see. The fee for a late filing is set by law and usually falls on the client, so a miss costs trust before it costs money. Firms stay on time by giving every return one task with a statutory due date, one owner, a recorded partner approval and filing proof.

Key takeaways

  • Late fees are small per day but run without a pause: for example ₹200 a day on a late TDS statement under the 1961 Act, and ₹100 a day per ROC form. Interest on unpaid GST is 18% a year.
  • Misses come from what the sheet cannot show: a silent client, a return waiting for review, a filing with no proof saved.
  • Track one task per client, return and period, with its due date, owner, stage and acknowledgement. Show partners the exceptions, not the full list.

Why do CA firms miss due dates when they track clients in Excel and WhatsApp?

A sheet shows the status somebody last typed, not what is stuck. Each staff member also chases papers in their own client chats, so nobody else sees that a client has gone quiet until the due date is close. We found no independent survey of how often Indian firms miss dates or how they track them, so we quote no rate.

Three gaps repeat in how a small firm works day to day:

  • Silent clients. Papers are asked for in a chat, and the sheet says “waiting” for days without anyone being told.
  • Review by walking over. A return waits for a partner and nothing records how long, or whether the partner looked at it before filing.
  • Proof saved somewhere. The portal acknowledgement ends up in email or a folder, and the sheet just says “filed”.

What does a late GSTR-3B cost?

A late GSTR-3B carries a late fee and, if tax is unpaid, interest. The central part of the fee is ₹25 a day, or ₹10 a day when the return is nil (Notification 76/2018-Central Tax). The state tax part is set the same way, so the usual total is double; confirm for your state.

From June 2021, the central part is capped by turnover: ₹250 for a nil return, ₹1,000 up to ₹1.5 crore of preceding-year turnover, and ₹2,500 above that up to ₹5 crore (Notification 19/2021-Central Tax). Interest on late-paid tax is 18% a year (Notification 13/2017-Central Tax, under section 50).

Example, not a real client: a client with turnover under ₹1.5 crore files GSTR-3B 10 days late, with ₹1,00,000 of tax paid 30 days late. Late fee: 10 days × ₹25 central + ₹25 state = ₹500. Interest: ₹1,00,000 × 18% × 30 / 365 = about ₹1,479. Check the real figures with your partner.

What does a late TDS statement cost?

Under section 234E of the Income-tax Act, 1961, a late TDS or TCS statement attracts a fee of ₹200 for each day of delay, capped at the tax deductible or collectible (Income Tax Department guidance, section 234E). A separate penalty under section 271H runs from ₹10,000 up to ₹1,00,000 (Income Tax Department reckoner). Interest on tax not deducted is 1% a month, and on tax deducted but not deposited 1.5% a month (Income Tax Department: interest and fees).

One caution. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. Its section 427 deals with this fee (CAclubindia, secondary source), and the same source notes that Finance Bill 2026 proposed to substitute the section. We could not confirm the current wording, so check which version applies to your period with your partner.

Example: a statement filed 10 days late is ₹200 × 10 = ₹2,000, unless the tax deducted is lower, in which case the fee stops at that amount.

What does a late ROC filing cost?

For the annual return (MGT-7) and financial statements (AOC-4), the Companies Act, 2013 allows late filing on an additional fee of not less than ₹100 a day, and MCA has applied ₹100 a day to these forms from 1 July 2018 (TaxGuru report on the 2018 amendment, secondary source). MCA’s General Circular 01/2026 is described as stating there is no upper limit on this fee (MCA circular; the file would not open for our tools, so we saw it in a search excerpt only). The fee is charged per form.

Example: a company files both AOC-4 and MGT-7 30 days late: 2 forms × 30 days × ₹100 = ₹6,000 in additional fees, plus the normal filing fees.

Who pays when a return is late?

The client pays the fee and interest. What the firm pays is harder to see, and we have no data on it: partner time on calls, a strained client, and the question of whether to absorb the fee. Some firms do. Your own books of the last year, with each fee and who paid it, will tell you the real figure.

How do firms keep every return on time?

They replace the sheet-plus-chats routine with a short, fixed routine for every return.

  1. One task per client, return and period. “GSTR-3B, client A, October 2026” is one row with its own state.
  2. A due date from the law. Record the source for each date, and record the notification when an extension moves it.
  3. One link for papers. The client uploads or emails; staff chase only those who have not sent, and a client with nothing to file says so once.
  4. Recorded partner approval. The return goes to a queue, oldest first, and is approved or sent back with a reason.
  5. Filing proof before closing. The acknowledgement number and file are attached; nothing closes as “filed” without them.
  6. Escalation by calendar day. The owner is reminded before the due date; managers and partners hear when it passes.

How does Docketbox do this?

Docketbox is built around those six steps. A task opens for each client, return and period, with its statutory due date and a checklist of papers. Clients send papers through a link or by email, without a login. Partners approve in a review queue that shows days waiting, and a return closes only with the acknowledgement number and file, both recorded in a tamper-evident log. Late tasks go to the owner, then managers, then partners, and a daily digest lists what is due, who is behind and which clients have not responded. It does not compute tax or file returns.

Questions CA partners ask

Who pays the late fee on a missed return, the CA firm or the client?

The statutes put the fee on the registered person, the deductor or the company that filed late, so the client. Whether the firm absorbs it is a business decision for the partners. Check your engagement terms with your partner.

Is there one due date I can track for every client?

No. Each return has its own due date, and extensions are notified for some forms and periods. Track the due date per client, return and period, and note the notification behind any change.

Does Docketbox file returns or calculate tax?

No. Docketbox tracks the work and the proof. Staff file on the portals and record the acknowledgement number and file in Docketbox. It does not compute tax or reconcile GST.
