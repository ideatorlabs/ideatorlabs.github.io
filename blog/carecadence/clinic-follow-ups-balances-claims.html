Why clinics lose follow-ups, balances and claims, and how to keep each one moving

Give every recall, balance and claim an owner, a due date and proof, until a visit, payment or settlement closes it.

In this article
  1. Why do clinics lose money on recalls, balances and claims?
  2. Do patients come back for a recall without a reminder?
  3. What should a clinic record when the doctor says “come back in six months”?
  4. How do you chase an unpaid balance without straining the relationship?
  5. Why do insurer and TPA claims stall, and what should a clinic track?
  6. What does a weekly routine look like?
  7. Questions owners ask

A clinic loses this money for one reason. A recall (“come back in six months”), a balance (“I’ll pay next visit”) and an insurer claim (“sent”) each start as a sentence, and nobody owns the sentence afterwards. The fix is to track each one as an item with one person responsible, a due date, and proof that closes it: the next visit, the payment or the settlement. This article is about the desk and the billing, not clinical care. When a patient should return is the doctor’s decision.

Key takeaways

  • Recalls, unpaid balances and insurer claims leak for the same reason: no owner, no due date, nothing that tells you when one has slipped.
  • An item is closed only by a recorded fact: a visit, a payment or a settlement. A promise to come is not a result.
  • For claims, record the age, the amount settled and the reason for every deduction, so you can contest the wrong ones and fix the paperwork behind the repeated ones.

Why do clinics lose money on recalls, balances and claims?

They are three leaks with one cause. A patient told to return is written in a register nobody opens on the right day. A part-paid bill sits in the billing app. A claim sits in a sheet until a deduction letter arrives. None has a due date that someone is told about, so each ages quietly. When the desk is also taking payments and answering the phone, the follow-up is the first thing to wait.

Do patients come back for a recall without a reminder?

Often not, and reminders help. Three sources, none of them a promise for your clinic:

  • Reminders in general. A Cochrane review of eight randomised trials (6,615 participants) found that text reminders raised attendance at healthcare appointments: 78.6% with a reminder against 67.8% without, across trials (risk ratio 1.14). The authors rate the evidence low to moderate (Cochrane review, 2013).
  • Dental recall in India. A randomised study of 224 dental patients over two years reported that a reminder application raised the recall rate from 21.1% to 37.8% (Stud Health Technol Inform, 2022). We read the abstract only, and it is one study.
  • Physiotherapy courses in India. In a record review of 909 patients at a 13-branch physiotherapy centre, 152 (17%) completed the treatment protocol (published 2020). It does not say why the others stopped.

What should a clinic record when the doctor says “come back in six months”?

  1. The doctor sets the interval at the end of the consultation, in days or months. The desk does not decide it.
  2. One line per recall: the patient, the service, the due date and who will call. Keep patient names and treatment details out of titles and shared notes.
  3. Consent for the message. Tell the patient what you will message them about and let them opt out. India’s DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with an eighteen-month phased period, and require a separate consent notice that is clear and states the specific purpose (PIB explainer, 17 November 2025). This is not legal advice.
  4. A morning list. Overdue first, then due today, then due this week. Each row shows the next action.
  5. Close it on a recorded visit. If the patient declines or opts out, close it with that reason.

You can send from the clinic’s own phone. A WhatsApp click-to-chat link in the form https://wa.me/<number>?text=<message> opens the chat with the message ready (WhatsApp Help Center).

Example message (adapt it): “Hello, this is [clinic name]. Dr [name] had asked you to come back around now. Would Thursday or Saturday suit you?” Leave treatment details out of the message.

How do you chase an unpaid balance without straining the relationship?

Agree a date when the balance arises, not a vague “next visit”. Staged treatments such as implants, braces or a course of sessions are where balances build up.

  1. Record the amount, the bill number and the promised date on the day.
  2. On that date, send one polite message with the amount and the bill number.
  3. If the patient names a new date, record it and move the due date. Do not chase before it.
  4. If it stays unpaid, tell the owner. Only the owner or billing writes a balance off, with a reason.

Example, not a result: if 40 patients fall due in a month, a usual bill is ₹1,500 and 10 more come back because someone called, that is ₹15,000. If six balances of ₹2,500 are collected after a dated reminder, that is another ₹15,000. Use your own numbers.

Why do insurer and TPA claims stall, and what should a clinic track?

Claims stall when nobody ages them and nobody records why money was cut. Nationally, IRDAI’s annual report figures for FY2023-24, as quoted by LocalCircles, show insurers disallowed about ₹15,100 crore and repudiated ₹10,937 crore of health claims, with 71.3% paid by value (LocalCircles, 7 March 2026). These are all health claims, mostly hospital ones, not a clinic benchmark. In the same survey of 18,989 claimants, 15% said a claim was rejected for invalid reasons and 29% said it was only partly approved for invalid reasons. Those answers are self-reported.

IRDAI’s Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (29 May 2024) tells insurers to decide a cashless request within one hour, give final authorisation within three hours of a discharge request from the hospital, and not repudiate any claim without committee approval. If a claim is repudiated or partly disallowed, the details must be conveyed to the claimant with reference to the specific policy terms (IRDAI). How it applies to your clinic depends on your arrangement with the insurer or TPA, so ask them.

For every claim, track the insurer or TPA, the date sent, the amount claimed, its age (0–30, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90 days), the amount settled, and the deduction with its reason. A deduction with a readable reason can be contested or fixed at source. Without one, it is just a smaller payment.

What does a weekly routine look like?

  • Every morning, 15 minutes: one list, past-date items first, with the next action on each row.
  • When an item slips: the owner is told the next day, and again after a week. A claim is flagged again at 60 and 90 days old.
  • On hold: a disputed bill or an open insurer query can be paused with a reason, so honest waits do not read as failures.
  • Weekly, for the owner: rupees recovered, counted only from closed items; what slipped and with whom.

Questions owners ask

How often should a clinic message a patient who is due back?

We found no measured rule for Indian clinics. A common starting point is one message on the due date and one a week later, then record the outcome and stop after a decline or opt-out. The doctor sets the follow-up interval, not the desk.

Do we need the WhatsApp Business API to send these messages?

No. WhatsApp click-to-chat links open a chat with a ready message from the clinic’s own phone, one patient at a time. The API is for approved templates and automatic sending, which most small clinics do not need to start.

Can a spreadsheet do this?

Yes, if every row has an owner, a due date, a status and the proof that closed it, and someone opens it every day. It usually fails when nobody is told that a row has slipped past its date.
