Your D2C brand’s real problem isn’t ads — it’s true margin per order

Before you change the ads, find out what each order really leaves you with.

In this article
  1. Why margin per order is hard to see
  2. How to work out true margin per order
  3. What one data layer does
  4. How to start
  5. How we can help

For many D2C brands, the thing holding growth back is not the ads. It is not knowing how much each order really earns once product cost, packing, shipping, payment fees, returns and ad spend are taken out. Until you can see that margin per order, by product and by channel, you can’t tell which ads and products deserve more money.

Key takeaways

  • Revenue is easy to see. What is left per order after every cost usually isn’t, because the costs sit in different tools and invoices.
  • Each ad platform reports its own conversions, so adding them up can count the same order more than once.
  • Bring orders, costs and ad spend into one place, work out margin per order, and only then decide where to spend.

Why margin per order is hard to see

Your store shows revenue. Meta and Google each show the sales they think they drove. Courier and warehouse charges, RTO (return to origin) fees and payment gateway fees arrive as separate invoices or sit in spreadsheets. Nobody sees all of it for one order.

  • Ad platforms overlap. Each platform takes credit using its own rules, so the same order can show up as a win in more than one dashboard. Spend gets moved on numbers that don’t add up.
  • Per-order costs are hidden. A best seller can lose money on every order once shipping, discounts and returns are counted, and you won’t see it from revenue alone.
  • Cash gets stuck in the wrong stock. Without margin by product, it is easy to run out of the products that make money while holding too much of the ones that don’t.
  • Reports take hours to build. Someone exports CSV files from each tool every week and stitches them together by hand, and the sheet is out of date soon after.

How to work out true margin per order

Start with what the customer paid, then take out every cost that order caused:

  • Money received, after discounts (and before GST)
  • Minus product cost and packing
  • Minus shipping, and the payment gateway fee
  • Minus the ad spend it took to win that order
  • Minus an allowance for returns and RTO, based on your own return history

For example (made-up numbers, to show the sum, all before GST): an order brings in ₹1,000 after discount. Product cost is ₹400, packing ₹30, shipping ₹70 and the gateway fee ₹20. Ad spend per order on that channel is ₹250. If 1 in 10 orders comes back and each return costs you ₹150, that is ₹15 per order. The order leaves you ₹215, not the ₹1,000 the store dashboard shows. Run the same sum per product and per channel and the picture often changes.

What one data layer does

You don’t need another dashboard subscription. You need your numbers in one place:

  • Pull the data in. Orders from your store, fees from your payment gateway, charges from your courier or warehouse, and spend from your ad accounts, all into one database.
  • Work out margin per order every day, with the same rules each time, including returns.
  • Show what earns money. A simple view of which products and channels make money after all costs, so spend decisions rest on numbers you trust.

How to start

  1. Take last month’s orders and the matching invoices from your courier, gateway and ad accounts.
  2. Work out margin per order for your top-selling products using the sum above.
  3. Compare it with what your ad dashboards say each channel earned.
  4. Automate the sum only once you trust it by hand.
