## SECTION B — Insight / POV posts (between Leak Reports)

### Insight 1 — the value gap, reframed for leaders
```
The uncomfortable stat every board should sit with:

88% of companies now use AI in at least one function. Only about 1 in 18 can point to real profit from it.
(McKinsey, The State of AI 2025)

The winners aren't the ones with the most AI. They're the ones who did the boring work first: found a specific, expensive problem, measured it, and pointed AI at exactly that.

"We're using AI" is not a strategy. "We cut our order-processing cost 40% and here's the number" is.

Adoption is table stakes now. Value capture is the whole game.
```

### Insight 2 — what "AI strategy" should actually mean
```
Most "AI strategy" decks are a list of tools. That's not a strategy — that's shopping.

A real one answers three questions, in order:
1. Where does this business actually lose time and money? (measure)
2. Which of those are rule-based enough for AI to help? (qualify)
3. What's the smallest pilot that proves value in 30 days? (sequence)

Start with the leak, not the model. The tool is the last decision, not the first — which is exactly backwards from how most AI projects begin (and why ~93% never show real EBIT impact).

Where would you point AI first if you knew exactly what it cost you today?
```

### Insight 3 — certainty is the product
```
"We'll just do it internally."

Every internal improvement project described this way that I've seen was still on the roadmap a year later. Not because the team wasn't capable — because it was never anyone's only job.

That's what a fixed-scope engagement actually buys: not expertise you lack, but certainty it happens — in weeks, with a number attached, whether or not it would ever have climbed your own priority list.

The deliverable isn't a report. It's certainty.
```

### Insight 4 — the India-specific take (Track-A audience)
```
Indian SMBs don't have an AI-awareness problem. They have an AI-application problem.

(NASSCOM / Meta; Google–India SME Forum)
```

### Insight 5 — cost of poor pricing / discounting
```
A 1% price increase is worth ~8% of operating profit (McKinsey). A 1% discount you didn't need to give costs you the same 8% — in reverse.

Most businesses obsess over cutting costs (1% of materials = ~5% profit) and chasing volume (1% more = ~3% profit), while the most powerful lever — price — runs on autopilot and quiet discounting.

You're likely managing your third-most-powerful profit lever hardest, and your most-powerful one least.

Where does your pricing get decided — on a strategy, or in the moment a customer pushes back?
```

## SECTION C — The Leak Report series (the signature franchise)

*Format repeats: headline stat → THE PATTERN → THE NUMBER → HOW TO SEE IT → THE FIX → a question. One per week. These are your recurring, most-valuable content — they double as sales collateral.*

### Leak Report No. 1 — Customer profitability
```
📊 THE LEAK REPORT — No. 1
15–30% of your customers are probably unprofitable. And you can't name which ones.

THE PATTERN
Most companies measure profit at the company level, never per customer. So the accounts quietly losing money hide inside a healthy-looking P&L — often the "big" ones everyone's proud of.

THE NUMBER
In a typical mid-market book, the bottom 20% of customers erase 10–30% of total profit. Repricing or exiting them can lift net margin double digits — with zero new sales.

HOW TO SEE IT (an afternoon)
1. Revenue per customer, last 12 months
2. Fully-loaded cost to serve each (delivery + support + discounts + payment terms)
3. Rank by contribution margin
4. Look hard at the bottom decile

THE FIX
Reprice, restructure, or exit the losers. Redirect freed capacity to the top quartile.

Have you ever measured margin per customer — or only at the company level?
```

### Leak Report No. 2 — Pricing drift
```
📊 THE LEAK REPORT — No. 2
1% of price = ~8% of operating profit (McKinsey). When did you last touch yours?

THE PATTERN
Pricing is the highest-leverage number in a business and the most neglected. Costs creep up; prices don't follow; discounts become the silent default.

THE NUMBER
For a typical operating margin, a 1% lift in realized price drops ~8% to the bottom line — no new customers, no new cost. (McKinsey, "The Power of Pricing")

HOW TO SEE IT
1. List price vs. realized price, by product & segment
2. The gap = your discount leakage
3. Date of your last deliberate increase?
4. Segment by willingness-to-pay, not habit

THE FIX
Structured repricing: raise where value justifies, tighten discount authority, arm sales with scripts.

When did your business last raise prices on purpose?
```

### Leak Report No. 3 — Manual back-office
```
📊 THE LEAK REPORT — No. 3
20–40% of your admin payroll is spent on work AI can now do.

THE PATTERN
Document handling, re-keying between systems, status-chasing, reconciliations. No judgment required — just salaried hours, every day, invisibly.

THE NUMBER
20–40% of back-office time is rule-based work modern automation + document AI can absorb, often with payback in months. (Early India pilots: 20–30% operational cost decline — NASSCOM.)

HOW TO SEE IT
1. Team logs tasks for one week
2. Tag each: "needs judgment" vs "rule-based"
3. Rule-based hours × loaded cost
4. That annual number is the opportunity

THE FIX
Automate the rule-based tier first — highest volume, lowest risk. Keep humans on judgment.

Roughly what share of your team's week is copy-paste-and-reconcile work?
```

### Leak Report No. 4 — Cash conversion
```
📊 THE LEAK REPORT — No. 4
Six or seven figures are trapped in your business right now. It's called the cash conversion cycle.

THE PATTERN
Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. Money sticks in three places: invoices sent late, collections chased inconsistently, inventory ordered "to be safe."

THE NUMBER
Tightening the cycle commonly frees hundreds of thousands to millions in cash — capital you already earned but can't use.

HOW TO SEE IT
Cash Conversion Cycle = DSO + DIO − DPO
→ DSO: days to collect
→ DIO: days inventory sits
→ DPO: days you take to pay
Shorten the first two, sensibly extend the third.

THE FIX
Auto-invoice the day work completes. Collections on a cadence, not a whim. Right-size inventory with real demand data.

What's your average days-to-collect? ("Not sure" is itself the finding.)
```

### Leak Report No. 5 — Customer-service load
```
📊 THE LEAK REPORT — No. 5
30–60% of your support cost goes to answering the same questions over and over.

THE PATTERN
Volume grows, so teams add headcount. But a large share of contacts are repeat, rule-based questions — exactly what AI now handles, with hard cases escalated to humans.

THE NUMBER
AI deflection, drafting and triage routinely cut cost-per-contact 30–60% while holding or improving CSAT — customers get faster answers.

HOW TO SEE IT
1. Tag one week of tickets by root cause
2. Pareto them — top 5 reasons are usually 60%+ of volume
3. How many are "look up and reply" vs "think and decide"?

THE FIX
Deflect + draft the repetitive tier; route the rest to humans with AI-assisted answers.

What's the #1 question your support team answers most?
```

### Leak Report No. 6 — Shelfware
```
📊 THE LEAK REPORT — No. 6
Most teams use ~20% of the software they pay for — and pay again in manual workarounds.

THE PATTERN
ERP/CRM gets bought, half-configured, lightly trained — then the team reverts to spreadsheets. Now you pay twice: license + the manual work the tool was meant to remove.

THE NUMBER
Typical feature utilization sits near 20%. The gap isn't the software — it's adoption, configuration, and the workflows around it.

HOW TO SEE IT
1. List every SaaS tool + annual cost
2. Ask the team what % they actually use
3. Count the shadow spreadsheets beside the "system of record"

THE FIX
Before buying anything new: configure, automate, drive adoption of what you own. Cheapest capability upgrade there is.

How many spreadsheets run next to a system that was supposed to replace them?
```
