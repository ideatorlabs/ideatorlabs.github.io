## Who it is for: Brands that sell on their own store

- Brands on Shopify with Meta and Google ad spend
- Brands with a large share of cash-on-delivery orders
- Founders, marketers and ops teams who own the fixes

## The problem: Losses hide behind ad-platform ROAS and COD returns

- COD orders come back far more often than prepaid ones, and each return costs shipping both ways.
- Ad platforms report ROAS before returns, so campaigns below break-even keep running.
- Profit is worked out in a spreadsheet that is a week behind, and nobody owns the fix.

## How Profora works

1. **Bring in the week's numbers.** Shopify, courier and ad exports are imported each week; Profora keeps weekly totals per product, pincode area, campaign and store.
2. **Find the leaks.** Products and pincode areas that lose money on settled orders, and campaigns whose ROAS is below the store's break-even.
3. **Give each leak an owner.** Products go to a founder, campaigns to the marketer, pincode areas to operations, each with a deadline.
4. **Record the fix with proof.** The owner picks the fix and attaches a screenshot or a written note. Late items escalate to the founders, then to accounts.
5. **Check that it paid back.** Later settled weeks are compared with the weeks before the fix. Only those numbers close a leak.

## How your data is handled

Each brand's workspace is kept apart from every other. Profora stores weekly totals per product, pincode area, campaign and store, product costs, and each leak's history in a log that cannot be edited. The uploaded files are read in memory and dropped; single orders and buyers' names, emails, phones and addresses are never stored. Data comes in from exports today; Shopify, courier and ad connections are not live yet.
