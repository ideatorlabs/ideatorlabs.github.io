## The problem: Package use and commission rest on trust

- A package session can be taken off without the client in the chair.
- Clients drift away because nobody has a list of who is due back.
- Month-end commission is worked out in Excel, and the arguments follow.

## How Chairside works

1. **The client confirms each package use.** The desk shows a QR code or sends a WhatsApp link; the client taps yes or no on their own phone. A refusal or no answer becomes a check for the outlet manager.
2. **The day's bills become checked lines.** The manager loads the POS sales report each day; every bill line becomes a checked service or product sale.
3. **The desk works the due-back list.** Clients due back for a service, and packages about to expire, each with a WhatsApp message ready. A client counts as back only on a checked visit.
4. **Commission comes from checked work.** Per-service rules or a monthly target with slabs. Stylists dispute a line from their phone; the owner approves each month, and the statement is frozen.
5. **The owner reads each outlet every morning.** Packages owed, unconfirmed uses, overdue due-backs, disputes, and patterns worth a look, with escalation when deadlines pass.

## How your data is handled

Records are kept in the chain's own database, and each business and outlet sees only its own data; stylists never see client phone numbers. Every package sale, use, client answer and approved statement goes into an append-only, hash-chained log. Messages to clients are drafts the desk sends from its own WhatsApp; nothing is sent to clients from our side. Past clients and packages are loaded once from your POS export.
