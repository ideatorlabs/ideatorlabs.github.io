# Docketbox by Ideator Labs

> Docketbox gives a CA practice one task for every client, return and period, such as GSTR-3B for October or a TDS statement for Q2, with its statutory due date and the papers it needs. Each task has an owner and moves from waiting on the client to documents received, in progress, partner review, approved and filed, and it closes only with the acknowledgement number and file. Late tasks go to the managers and partners on fixed days, and partners get a digest every morning.

## Who it is for: Practices that file recurring returns for their clients

- CA and accounting practices filing GST, TDS, income tax and ROC returns for their clients
- Teams of managers, accountants and article assistants who chase papers over WhatsApp and email
- Partners who need to know what is due, late or waiting for their review without asking around

## The problem: Where compliance work slips in a practice

- Papers arrive late and in pieces, and each staff member's chats are the only record of who has been asked.
- Status lives in spreadsheets kept by hand, so partners find late work close to the due date. A late GSTR-3B costs the client ₹50 a day in late fee, and a late TDS statement ₹200 a day.
- Nothing records whether a partner reviewed a return before it was filed, or where its acknowledgement was saved.

## How Docketbox works

1. **Tasks open by themselves.** When a period ends, Docketbox opens a task for every client and return the firm handles, with the statutory due date and its source, the checklist of papers and the client's staff member as owner. Extensions the firm enters, with the notification, move every open task of that form and period.
2. **Clients send papers.** Each client has a private link with no sign-in that lists what is needed. They upload photos or PDFs from a phone, email attachments to their own address, or say there is nothing to send for the period. Reminder emails go out on the firm's schedule until the papers are in.
3. **Work and partner review.** Staff start the task and send it for review. Partners and managers see a review queue, oldest first, with how long each return has waited, and approve it for filing or send it back with what to fix.
4. **Filed with proof.** The task closes only with the acknowledgement number and the acknowledgement file from the portal. Both are hashed into the task's log, and the client's page shows the return as filed.
5. **Escalation and the morning digest.** The task's owner is reminded three days before the due date and on it, managers hear the day after, partners three days after. Each morning partners get what is due this week, what waits for review, overdue work by person, clients not responding and the per-day late fees already running.

## How your data is handled

Each practice's clients, tasks and files are kept apart from every other practice's, and every page checks that a record belongs to the practice before showing it. A client's link shows only that client's own checklist; nothing can be downloaded through it. Uploaded files are checked by their content, not their name. Every task move, due-date change, escalation and filing goes into an append-only log in which each entry is hashed with the one before, so a changed or deleted entry shows; the acknowledgement number and file are hashed into it. Docketbox tracks work and proof: it does not compute tax, file returns or connect to the tax portals, and nothing is sent anywhere except the reminder and alert emails the firm sets up. A practice can export everything as one file, or close and delete its data in one step.
